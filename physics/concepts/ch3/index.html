Overview · 개요

Gauss’s law links net electric flux through a closed surface to enclosed charge QencQ_{\mathrm{enc}}Qenc​. It is equivalent to Coulomb’s law but shines when symmetry makes ∮E⃗⋅dA⃗\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A∮E⋅dA trivial. Coulomb integration over distributions is general but heavy; Gauss is the fast path for spheres, long wires, and large sheets.

Gauss’s law · 가우스 법칙

∮E⃗⋅dA⃗=Qencε0\oint \vec E \cdot d\vec A = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}∮E⋅dA=ε0​Qenc​​

Zero enclosed charge · 포함 전하 0

Gaussian surface with no net charge inside: ∮E⃗⋅dA⃗=0\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A = 0∮E⋅dA=0. Not the same as E=0E=0E=0 everywhere on the surface — field lines can enter and leave. Example: surface that encloses only vacuum in a multipole field.

Conductors in equilibrium — proofs in words · 도체 평형 (논증)

1. E=0E = 0E=0 inside bulk metal

Proof by contradiction: If E⃗≠0\vec E \ne 0E=0, free electrons accelerate and move until they cancel the field. Static equilibrium ⇒ no net motion ⇒ E=0E=0E=0 inside.

2. Excess charge on surface

Take a small Gaussian pillbox half in metal, half in vacuum. Inside metal E=0E=0E=0 on the inward face → flux through pillbox from interior is 0 → enclosed charge in bulk is 0. So volume charge = 0; extra charge sits on surface.

3. EEE perpendicular to surface

If E⃗\vec EE had a tangential component, surface charges would slide along the surface → not equilibrium. Hence just outside, E⃗⊥\vec E \perpE⊥ surface.

4. Field magnitude outside flat patch

Pillbox through surface with uniform σ\sigmaσ: flux mainly from outer cap, EA=σA/ε0EA = \sigma A/\varepsilon_0EA=σA/ε0​:

E=σε0E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}E=ε0​σ​

Caveat: Joined dissimilar metals (Pb/Ag) can have interface fields at equilibrium without net charge — different work functions / resistivity (lecture example).

내부 E=0E=0E=0: 아니면 자유 전하가 계속 이동. 여분 전하 표면: 금속 속 작은 가우스면 → 내부 전하 0. EEE 표면 수직: 접선 성분 있으면 표면 전하 이동. 바로 밖 E=σ/ε0E=\sigma/\varepsilon_0E=σ/ε0​. 이종 금속 접합면은 특수 케이스.

Flux through arbitrary closed surfaces · 임의 닫힌면

Two surfaces SSS and S′S'S′ enclosing the same charge QQQ have the same flux Q/ε0Q/\varepsilon_0Q/ε0​ even if one is a sphere and one is a lumpy blob. Lecture: flux depends on topology (what charge is inside), not on detailed geometry.

If no charge is inside, flux is zero — e.g. Gaussian surface in empty space between +Q+Q+Q and −Q-Q−Q of a dipole (field lines enter and leave).

같은 QQQ를 감싸면 플럭스 동일(구든 복잡한 형태든). 내부 전하 없으면 총 플럭스 0 — 쌍극자 사이 공간 등.

Pillbox at conductor surface · 도체 표면 pillbox

Imagine a thin box: one face in air, one face inside metal (where E=0E=0E=0). Enclosed charge = σA\sigma AσA. Outward flux only from external cap:

∮E⃗⋅dA⃗=σAε0=EA⇒E=σε0\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A = \frac{\sigma A}{\varepsilon_0} = EA \quad\Rightarrow\quad E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}∮E⋅dA=ε0​σA​=EA⇒E=ε0​σ​

This is the standard exam derivation for field just outside a conductor with surface charge density σ\sigmaσ.

**pillbox**: 공기쪽 면만 플럭스 → **$E=\sigma/\varepsilon_0$**. 시험에 자주 나오는 **도체 표면** 유도.

Immersed conductor slab · 외부 장 속 도체

External field (blue lines in lecture) polarizes a conductor: surface charges create canceling internal field. At equilibrium, net EEE inside bulk = 0; excess charge on faces. This is the macroscopic picture before Ch.4 equipotential language.

외부 장 → 표면 전하 분극 → 내부 E=0E=0E=0. Ch.4 등전위와 연결.
