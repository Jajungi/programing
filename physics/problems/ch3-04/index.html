Why is $\vec E = 0$ inside a conductor in equilibrium?

도체 내부 전기장이 0인 이유

Explain in words (and with one sentence of Gauss’s-law reasoning) why the static electric field must vanish everywhere inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium.

Answer
Free charges rearrange until internal E=0E=0E=0; Qenc=0Q_\mathrm{enc}=0Qenc​=0 inside
한글
재배치 후 내부 E=0E=0E=0
  1. Mobility. Conduction electrons (or ions) are free to move inside a conductor until no net force remains on them.
  2. If 0 inside. Charges would accelerate and redistribute — not equilibrium.
  3. Final state. Excess charge resides on the surface (or at interfaces); interior is neutral on average.
  4. Gauss argument. Any Gaussian surface fully inside has Qenc=0Q_\mathrm{enc}=0Qenc​=0, so ∮E⃗⋅dA⃗=0\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A = 0∮E⋅dA=0; with symmetry arguments, E=0E=0E=0 throughout the bulk.
  5. Note. This fails for time-varying fields (displacement current) — here we assume electrostatics.
